The 2026 Challenger Conference returned to Bangor for the fourth time this September (7–11 September), hosting nearly
200 marine scientists across oral
sessions, posters, and Special Interest Group meetings, making it one of the UK’s largest gatherings of marine
scientists. Keynotes included Emma McKinley on ocean literacy, Gerard
McCarthy on the AMOC, and Kate Hendry on polar biogeochemistry, alongside the Challenger Medal lecture by our University
of Liverpool colleague Prof. Ric
Williams on “Oceans and climate in a warming world.” Our BCP session sat among a rich programme spanning polar oceans,
shelf seas, mixing and turbulence, and marine ecosystems.

## Our presence at the conference

PRECOG had a strong showing, with four contributions spanning posters and an oral talk in the “Biological
Carbon Pump (BCP) & Ocean Carbon Storage” session.

### Sam Ditkosvsky (oral)

Sam took over the stage on Tuesday during the “Biological Carbon Pump & Ocean Carbon Storage” session
and presented a talk titled "Understanding carbon sequestration times in transient climate simulations" where he
addressed key differences between Csoft and Cseq depending on how carbon sequestration timescales are estimated
(i.e., from a water parcel ventilation perspective or return time from the interior to the surface) and how these evolve
under transient climate forcing, connecting BCP efficiency to time‑dependent changes in circulation and biogeochemistry.

### Jamie Wilson (poster)

Jamie presented a poster titled "Linking organic matter cycling to carbon storage: a globally integrated steady‑state
model of the Biological Carbon Pump". This work presents a steady‑state, globally integrated framework that ties organic
matter cycling to carbon storage,
helping to bridge mechanistic BCP representations with large‑scale carbon budgets.

### Leo Bertini (poster)

Leo presented a poster titled "Isolating Biogeochemical and Circulation Controls on Ocean Carbon Storage by the
Biological Carbon Pump", where he featured for the first time the Python package he's been working on. It uses a 1‑D
framework to solve POC fluxes
across different ocean biogeochemical models, which can then be used to reconstruct biology-driven POC fluxes and
further isolate
circulation‑driven uncertainty from coupled model groundtruths, providing a basis to interpret inter‑model spread and
model‑intrinsic
biases in biologically mediated ocean carbon storage.

### Chesca Pharos (poster)

Chesca presented a poster titled "Exploring circulation‑linked proxies for ocean carbon sequestration"
showcasing the latest work on exploring proxies that link ocean circulation diagnostics to carbon sequestration, aiming
to identify observable indicators of BCP strength and variability.

## Thanks and next steps

Thanks to the Challenger Society and our Bangor University colleagues for hosting a vibrant meeting.
We’re grateful to everyone who stopped by our posters and joined the discussions — the feedback will directly
shape the next stage of the work we do as part of the PRECOG project.

Challenger 2026 was a fantastic opportunity to share our latest work on the biological carbon pump, connect with the UK
marine community, and celebrate progress across the project. It was also a particularly enjoyable meeting on a personal
level. We were very excited to be reunited in Bangor, to celebrate
Ric’s medal lecture, and to catch up with many of our fellow UK-based marine scientists. 

The traditional conference
dinner was another highlight, with the University of Liverpool very well represented both around the table (photo shown
below) and, later in the evening, on the dancefloor.
